PROJECT — TINY PLANES (GAME)
============================
Type: GAME (mobile). Working title: "Tiny Planes".

This is a game project, distinct from Kim's utility apps (e.g. the Flight Tracker).
Spiritual sibling of the old flag-quiz app: quick, scoreable, time + accuracy.

CORE FRAME (locked with Kim, 2026-10-08)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
- CASUAL "time-killer": a game you play for a minute or two while waiting for something.
- ROUND LENGTH: two minutes or less per round. This is the organising constraint.
  (UNDER REVIEW — may shorten; see PACING REVISION.)
- FEEL: quick dopamine / endorphin hit. Pick-up-and-play, one-thumb.
- "TINY": cute tiny planes AND a tiny, minimal game (both senses — Kim said "yes" to all).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
BUSINESS MODEL (locked)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
- FREE to play, no upfront cost.
- Revenue = ADVERTISEMENTS only, at launch.
- Ads must be SMALL and UNOBTRUSIVE and targeted — NOT the 30-second forced
  interstitials that annoy people. Think small-format, low-friction ad units
  that generate a revenue stream without wrecking the quick-play feel.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
PROGRESSION / DIFFICULTY (locked)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
- Tetris-style ramp: starts easy, gets harder and faster as you go.
- Each round: player can either SAVE PROGRESS from earlier, or PROCEED DIRECTLY
  and push into harder and harder play.

PACING REVISION (NOT YET BUILT — planned, 2026-10-08)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Problem from playtest: game felt a bit slow; 2 min may be too long; ramp too gentle.
Planned fixes (two levers, likely BOTH):
1. SHORTER ROUND: try ~90s or even ~60s so there's no dead air. (Exact value TBD.)
2. PUNCHIER RAMP: steeper, possibly ACCELERATING curve rather than linear; slightly
   more cloud up front so the player is engaged from the off; back half genuinely
   frantic. (This also makes Endless mode more exciting later.)
3. PLAYER-DRIVEN SPEED (see CONTROL SCHEME throttle) folds into pacing: "slow"
   becomes a player choice, not a limitation.
4. [PROPOSED] Put ROUND LENGTH in ONE config constant and express every in-round
   timing as a FRACTION of it (see DESIGN NOTES → RELATIVE TIMINGS), so trying
   60s vs 90s vs 120s is a one-number change.
5. [OPEN] TIME vs DISTANCE round — see DESIGN NOTES. Must be decided alongside the
   throttle, because it changes what "faster" means.

CONTROL SCHEME (locked with Kim, 2026-10-08; throttle addition NOT YET BUILT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
- FLOATING VIRTUAL JOYSTICK: the joystick appears wherever the player's thumb
  first touches down, so the player chooses where to place it (no fixed anchor). [BUILT]
- Analog / continuous steering — suits threading tightening gaps (VFR) and
  chasing clouds (IFR). [BUILT]
- LEFT-THUMB NUDGE: gently SUGGEST the LEFT thumb (hint/ghost/glow + one-time
  prompt); works anywhere, not hard-locked. [BUILT — "Steer with your left thumb"]
- LEFT / RIGHT THROTTLE (NEW, NOT YET BUILT):
    * UP/DOWN still steer vertically.
    * Fly HARD RIGHT = INCREASE speed; fly HARD LEFT = DECREASE speed.
    * This replaces the old pure "auto-forward, no throttle" rule: the plane still
      always moves forward at a MINIMUM speed, but the player modulates it.
    * MUST keep a SPEED FLOOR so hard-left slows but NEVER reverses the plane.
      (Likely relevant to the backwards-landing bug — see LANDING FIX.)
    * [PROPOSED] Enforce the floor as a HARD CLAMP in the movement/scroll update
      (every frame, after all inputs + scripted sequences), NOT only in the
      joystick→throttle mapping. See DESIGN NOTES → SPEED FLOOR.
    * Pairs with scoring later: faster flying earns more → speed = the way to chase
      points, and hands pacing to the player.

PLANE TILT / PITCH ANIMATION (NEW, NOT YET BUILT — 2026-10-08)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: give the plane visual feedback that it's shifting direction / speed — makes
it feel alive and tells the player what their input is doing.
Mapping (confirmed with Kim; throttle axis is real-flight-accurate):
- VERTICAL STEERING:
    * Climbing / going UP        → pitch NOSE UP (lean back).
    * Descending / going DOWN    → pitch NOSE DOWN (lean forward).
- THROTTLE (speed) axis (real-flight-accurate, like a flare):
    * SLOWING DOWN (flaring)     → pitch NOSE UP.
    * SPEEDING UP                → pitch NOSE DOWN.
- Just a few degrees of rotation, EASED smoothly (not snappy).
- Layer ON TOP of the existing BOB animation (keep the bob — Kim likes it).
- When the two axes disagree (e.g. climbing while speeding up), BLEND between them
  so it looks natural.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
LANDING FIX (NOT YET BUILT — planned, 2026-10-08)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Bug: at the finish line the plane flew BACKWARDS (auto-land fighting the scroll;
forward speed likely went negative or scroll kept running during handover).
Fix plan:
- At the finish line, LOCK OUT throttle/steering input immediately (take controls
  away cleanly).
- Force forward speed to a FIXED, gentle positive landing value (never negative).
- Play the touchdown as a SCRIPTED sequence the player can't drag backwards.
- Then: hide joystick, round-over animation, round counter +1 (as today).
- [PROPOSED] The global SPEED FLOOR clamp (see DESIGN NOTES) also applies during
  landing, as a safety net in case the scripted sequence or scroll handover ever
  computes a negative speed again.
- [PROPOSED] Only ONE system moves the world during landing: either the scroll
  stops and the plane moves, or the plane holds and the scroll continues — never
  both fighting.

TUNING / NUMBERS v1 (locked with Kim, 2026-10-08 — VFR; tune by feel)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
NOTE: round length & ramp shape are under revision (see PACING REVISION).
CLOUD COVERAGE RAMP (over the round):
- Starts at 0% cover (clear blue skies) at take-off.
- Builds to 80–90% cover by the end.
- CRITICAL RULE: ALWAYS a guaranteed CLEAR PATH through — a navigable LABYRINTH,
  never a sealed wall. Failure should feel like the player's fault.
  * BUILD APPROACH: carve a SAFE CORRIDOR first, THEN fill cloud around it. Do NOT
    scatter clouds randomly (random scatter can seal the route at high coverage).

LIGHTNING / THUNDER CLOUDS (NOT YET BUILT):
- First exposure at ~45 seconds into the round (relative to final round length).
  [PROPOSED as fraction: 37.5% of the round — i.e. 45s of 120s.]
- Build to AT MOST 20–30% of screen AREA by the end.

ENERGY:
- Start at 100%. [BUILT: starts at 100%]
- Energy only DRAINS in VFR (no passive refill); recovery is via gas canisters only.
- NORMAL cloud contact: drains 10% PER SECOND (100 → 0 in 10s of continuous contact).
- THUNDER cloud contact: drains 30% PER SECOND (100 → 0 in ~3.3s). [NOT YET BUILT]

GAS CANISTERS (NOT YET BUILT — VFR booster, restores energy):
- Each restores +25% energy (capped at 100%).
- Three per round, placed at: 1:00, 1:30, 1:45 (rescale if round length changes).
  [PROPOSED as fractions: 50%, 75%, 87.5% of the round.]
- Design tension: sit in spots that cost some cloud contact to reach (risk/reward);
  cap at 100% nudges grabbing them when low.

- Between rounds: TAP TO LAUNCH the next (harder) round — not an automatic roll-on.

DESIGN NOTES (added 2026-10-08 — Claude's suggestions, NOT yet locked)
====================================================================
1. SPEED FLOOR = HARD CLAMP [PROPOSED]
   - The landing bug and the throttle share one root risk: forward speed going
     negative. Put a single clamp in the per-frame movement/scroll update:
       speed = max(SPEED_FLOOR, speed)   (applied AFTER input + scripted sequences)
   - This protects normal play, the throttle's hard-left, AND the landing handover.
   - The throttle mapping can still have its own floor, but the clamp is the
     guarantee.

2. RELATIVE TIMINGS [PROPOSED]
   - One constant: ROUND_LENGTH_SECONDS. Every in-round event is a fraction of it:
       * Thunder clouds first appear ......... 0.375 (45s of 120s)
       * Gas canister #1 ...................... 0.50  (1:00 of 2:00)
       * Gas canister #2 ...................... 0.75  (1:30 of 2:00)
       * Gas canister #3 ...................... 0.875 (1:45 of 2:00)
       * Coverage ramp 0% → 80–90% ............ 0.0 → 1.0
   - Result: switching 120s → 90s → 60s rescales everything automatically.
   - Check after shortening: at 60s, canister #3 lands at 52.5s and thunder at
     22.5s — confirm by feel that this still reads well, not too crowded.

3. TIME vs DISTANCE ROUND [OPEN — decide with Kim]
   - With a throttle, "round length" can mean two things:
       A) FIXED TIME (today): the finish line arrives at e.g. 90s regardless of
          speed. Flying fast covers more ground but doesn't end the round sooner;
          speed must be rewarded via SCORE.
       B) FIXED DISTANCE: the runway is a set distance away. Flying fast reaches it
          sooner — aggressive players get shorter, riskier rounds; cautious players
          get longer, safer ones. Pacing becomes fully player-owned.
   - Option B fits "slow becomes a player choice" (PACING #3) very naturally, but
     the ramp would then need to key off DISTANCE travelled, not time elapsed, and
     the timer UI would become a distance/progress bar.
   - Option A is simpler to build and keeps the "2:00" countdown UI.
   - Either is fine; the relative-timings constant (note 2) works for both if the
     fraction is "fraction of round completed" rather than "seconds elapsed".
